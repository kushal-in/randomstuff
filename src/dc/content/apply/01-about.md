# PC Deputy Coordinator Selections 2026

Welcome to the **Programming Club Deputy Coordinator Selections 2026**.

We're excited to have you here, and we're looking forward to seeing what you can build, solve, and figure out.

## About the Role

As a Deputy Coordinator, you'll work under the guidance of the Programming Club Coordinators throughout the year. The tenure will involve a mix of:

- Upskilling sessions in **Competitive Programming and Software Development**
- Participating in and working around programming contests
- Working with the rest of the PC team on club activities
- A few treats along the way
- Building and shipping a **mini project** towards the end of the tenure

The goal isn't to turn you into an expert overnight. It's to give you a group of people to learn with, work with, and get better alongside.

---

# Before You Begin

This application is intentionally **longer and harder in some places than what you may be used to**.

Don't worry about finishing everything.

We are **not expecting everyone to complete the entire application**. Some questions and problems are deliberately challenging, and that's okay. Even if you only get through a part of it, give it a genuine attempt.

What we're interested in is **how you approach problems**

A partially completed problem with a good approach tells us much more than a perfect solution you didn't write yourself.

### A few things to keep in mind

- **Do not use AI to solve the application.** This includes ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, or similar tools. You gain very little from doing so, and AI-generated solutions are usually quite easy to identify.
- Looking up **documentation, syntax, language references, or existing reference material is completely fine**. That's a normal part of programming.
- Don't be afraid to submit incomplete solutions. **Partial implementations and reasonable attempts count.**
- You can move on from a problem if you're stuck and come back to it later.
- Don't spend hours trying to perfect one question at the expense of everything else.
- Don't copy or submit someone else's solution.
- If something in the application is unclear, **ask us** rather than making assumptions.

Most importantly, **give it a try**. You don't need to be great at everything here.

# The Application

The application consists of two parts:

### 1. Competitive Programming
Problems covering problem-solving, algorithms, and implementation.

### 2. Software Development
A combination of programming fundamentals, reading/learning material, and implementation problems.

You're encouraged to attempt **both parts**. You don't need to be equally strong in both.

---

# Competitive Programming

The CP section is divided into **4 main sections + 1 bonus section**.

All CP questions will be hosted in a **Codeforces contest**, which will remain open until the application deadline.

- Try to complete all **4 main sections**.
- The **bonus section is optional**, and is meant for those who want to push a little further.
- Don't worry if you can't finish everything. Your approach and attempts matter.

## Codeforces Contests

We'll also consider your performance in the **next 3 Codeforces contests**.

Only your **best contest** will be considered, so one or two bad rounds won't hurt you.

Try to participate in at least one of them **live and rated**, rather than virtually.

### A few things to keep in mind

- Write your own code.
- Stuck on a problem? Submit what you have anyway.
- Partial solutions and good approaches still tell us something.
- Don't obsess over your rating or score. We're looking at the bigger picture.

---

# Software Development

The Development section has two main components:

### 1. Reading & Learning

You'll be introduced to a few C++ concepts through provided reading material:

- **Pointers & References**
- **Structs & Classes**

Each topic comes with some questions to help you keep track of what you've understood.

There is also a **bonus topic: Macros & Compilers**.

The bonus material is optional, but is a good opportunity to explore something beyond the core requirements.

### 2. Implementation

After the learning section, you'll work on a few implementation-based problems covering concepts such as:

- Linked lists, merge sort & inversion counting
- Binary Search Trees
- C++ classes and related concepts

These problems are hosted together in a **HackerRank contest**.

Detailed problem statements, starter code, and additional reading material will be provided where necessary.

**Don't worry if these problems look difficult. They're meant to be.**

---

# Submitting Your Application

Your application is submitted **directly through this website**.

Once you verify your email:

1. Your application will be automatically sent to us.
2. You'll have the option to **download a copy of your application**.
3. You can make changes and **resubmit as many times as you want** before the deadline.
4. Only your final submission before the deadline will be considered.

**Deadline: Sunday, October 25, 11:59 PM**

---

# One Last Thing

Don't treat this like an exam where you need to get everything right.

Some parts will be easy. Some will probably make you question why you opened this page in the first place.

That's intentional.

**Try things. Get stuck. Come back. Leave things incomplete if you have to.**

We care much more about **how you think and approach problems** than whether you manage to finish every single question.

Good luck, and have fun.
